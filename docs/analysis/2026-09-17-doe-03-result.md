# DOE 03 — per-case judge disagreement does not predict the aggregate floor

**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Pre-registration:** [../doe/03-noise-floor.md](../doe/03-noise-floor.md)
**Engine:** `4023875557346246656` (`gepa-c08-new-r1`), health gate passed at 60 probes
**Eval set:** `examples/multi_model_agents/eval_data/eval_cases.yaml`, 64 cases
**Pool:** 6 captures × 5 scorings = 30 scoring passes, **131.8 min**
**Scripts:** [`doe03_collect.py`](../../scripts/doe03_collect.py),
[`analyze_doe03.py`](../../scripts/analyze_doe03.py)

## Headline

**One of the two pre-registered predictions held and the other failed, and the failure is
the useful part.**

DOE 02 measured the judge disagreeing with itself, on byte-identical responses, on **0/64**
cases for `safety_v1` and **64/64** for `instruction_following_v1`. The pre-registered
inference was that `score_repeats` would therefore do nothing for safety and a great deal
for the holdout.

| metric | judge disagreement | predicted | **measured `score_repeats` exponent** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `safety_v1` | 0/64 | repeats useless | **0.02 — correct** |
| `instruction_following_v1` | 64/64 | repeats the best buy | **−0.06 — wrong** |

**A per-case disagreement rate predicts aggregate variance reduction in one direction only.**
0/64 correctly implies repeats buy nothing: there is no judge noise to average away. 64/64
does *not* imply repeats buy a lot, because **disagreements that cancel in the mean never
reach the aggregate score a campaign reads.** The judge moves every individual
`instruction_following_v1` case and the movements net out.

DOE 02 half-anticipated this — it reported its variance subtraction returning an impossible
386% and said plainly that the per-case rates, not the sds, were the robust statistic. The
over-reach was in the *interpretation* layered on top: treating a per-case rate as a claim
about aggregate variance. That inference is now withdrawn.

## The scaling exponents

Floor ~ k·n^−e, fitted 1→3 for `num_runs` and 1→5 for `score_repeats`, other knob held at 1.
**e = 0.5 is √n; e = 0 means the knob buys nothing.**

| metric | judge disagreement | `num_runs` e | `score_repeats` e | cheaper lever |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `safety_v1` | 0/64 | **0.58** | 0.02 | **runs** |
| `final_response_quality_v1` | 59.4% | 0.34 | **0.66** | **repeats** |
| `hallucination_v1` | 43.8% | 0.39 | 0.35 | either |
| `instruction_following_v1` | **64/64** | 0.20 | **−0.06** | **neither** |
| `tool_use_quality_v1` | 21.9% | −0.11 | 0.01 | already at its floor |

Two genuinely actionable rows, and one sobering one:

- **`safety_v1` beats √n on `num_runs` (0.58) and gets nothing from repeats.** Exactly the
  predicted shape, and it is the metric carrying the replicated five-arm effect.
- **`final_response_quality_v1` is the reverse** — repeats at 0.66 against runs at 0.34, so
  the cheaper knob is also the better one. Not predicted; it falls out of the same data.
- **`tool_use_quality_v1` is already at the floor** at (1,1) — 0.0074, the lowest of any
  metric — so both exponents are ~0 because there is nothing left to remove.

## Iso-cost verdict — what to actually set

At ~16.5 min per arm-side, three settings cost the same. Median |Δ|, 200 draws:

| metric | (3,1) *today's default* | (2,2) | (1,5) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `final_response_quality_v1` | 0.0135 | 0.0102 | **0.0068** |
| `hallucination_v1` | 0.0105 | 0.0106 | **0.0093** |
| `instruction_following_v1` | 0.0178 | **0.0152** | 0.0247 |
| `safety_v1` | **0.0082** | 0.0094 | 0.0149 |
| `tool_use_quality_v1` | 0.0084 | **0.0059** | 0.0074 |

**`(2,2)` is the best default.** It wins or ties on three metrics, is never worst, and is the
only setting that is not badly wrong somewhere — `(1,5)` is worst on the two metrics that
carry this repo's results, and `(3,1)` is worst on two others.

### Verdict stability, because a single ranking would not have been trustworthy

Re-run under five seeds. `(1,5)` is deterministic at this pool size (s=5 of 5 scorings is
not a sample), which is why its column never moves:

- **`(1,5)` is worst for both `safety_v1` and `instruction_following_v1` in 5/5 seeds.** That
  is the robust finding, and it is what sinks the prediction.
- `(3,1)` vs `(2,2)` for `safety_v1` **flips between seeds** (0.0081–0.0114 vs 0.0087–0.0111).
  Those two are not distinguishable at this pool size; do not rank them.

## `score_repeats` buys coverage, and that is its real job

It does not reduce the aggregate floor for most metrics. It does recover cases, decisively —
mean cases scored per metric, over the 6 captures:

| metric | s=1 | s=3 | s=5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `safety_v1` | **57.8** | 63.8 | **64.0** |
| `instruction_following_v1` | 62.2 | 64.0 | 64.0 |
| `final_response_quality_v1` | 62.0 | 64.0 | 64.0 |
| `hallucination_v1` | 63.8 | 64.0 | 64.0 |
| `tool_use_quality_v1` | **64.0** | 64.0 | 64.0 |

`safety_v1` recovers **+6.2 cases** — it loses ~10% of the eval set on a single scoring pass.
**s=3 is enough**; s=5 adds nothing beyond it.

`tool_use_quality_v1` sits at 64.0 even at s=1, independently replicating DOE 02 arm 4's
finding that the hardened judge prompt eliminated case loss (0/320 there, and here it never
drops a case across 30 passes).

## The holdout still cannot be resolved cheaply, and that is the campaign's most important result

`instruction_following_v1` has the **worst floor of any metric at every setting** (0.0152 at
best) and the **weakest response to both knobs** (0.20 and −0.06). It is the metric campaign
08 returned *unresolved* on, against a control drift of 0.022.

This campaign says that was not bad luck and is not fixable by spending more on either knob:
at ~16.5 min/side the holdout floor is 0.0152, and `cheapest_config_for()` will return `None`
for any target below roughly that. **A design that needs to resolve the holdout needs a
different instrument, not a bigger budget** — more eval cases, a pinned autorater, or the
metric promoted to a real criterion, which ADK currently forbids.

## Answering the original DOE 03

Its question was the one-knob version, and it has an answer:

- **The corrected `num_runs=3` floor is 0.0082–0.0178 per metric** (median |Δ|), against
  CLAUDE.md's remembered **0.011–0.014**. The old figure sits inside the new range but is
  **too optimistic for the holdout** (0.0178) and too pessimistic for safety (0.0082). A
  single pooled number was always going to be wrong for some metric; the per-metric table
  above replaces it.
- **√n does not hold, and the deviation is per metric** — 0.58 for safety down to −0.11 for
  tool use, against campaign 06's 1.5 and 0.9. Do not pass a single `scaling_exponent` for
  all metrics; that is why `minimum_detectable_effect()` takes one per call and refuses to
  default it.

## Limits, stated rather than buried

- **Cells are resampled from one pool of 6 captures and are not independent.** These are
  sampling distributions conditional on this pool.
- **`num_runs=3` has only 10 disjoint splits** — the most decision-relevant cell is the
  least precisely measured. Its ranking against (2,2) is not stable across seeds.
- **Within-engine only.** Excludes the deployment lottery DOE 01 measured at 0–100% reach,
  so these are not "how much does an arm vary" numbers.
- **Captures span 131.8 min**, interleaved with scoring rather than taken back-to-back. Real
  `eval_before`/`eval_after` are further apart, so these floors are still likely
  **optimistic**.
- **Cost constants drifted during the run.** DOE 02 measured 2.6 min/capture and 2.8
  min/scoring; this run saw ~3.9 and ~5. The iso-cost sets hold under proportional drift but
  not if scoring slows relative to inference — re-measure before trusting the ranking at a
  much later date.

## What to do with this

1. **Move the default to `(num_runs=2, score_repeats=2)`** from `(3,1)`. Same cost, never
   worst, and it fixes the two metrics `(3,1)` is worst on.
2. **Use `score_repeats≥3` whenever `safety_v1` matters**, for coverage rather than variance:
   it recovers 6 cases per side, and a case missing from one side is the dropout that
   silent-failures #5 showed reads as a prompt effect.
3. **Stop quoting a single noise floor.** Per-metric floors are now measured; wire them in.
4. **Do not design another campaign whose primary readout is `instruction_following_v1`**
   without changing the instrument.
